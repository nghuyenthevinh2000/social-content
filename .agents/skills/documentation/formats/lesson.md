# Lesson Format: Failure Mental Traps → Practical Solutions

Use this format when documenting practitioner lessons, post-mortems, retrospectives, strategic teardowns, and business playbooks where the primary goal is helping the reader unlearn costly mistakes and adopt proven operating frameworks.

---

## Core Structural Principle

> **Crucial Rule:** Place the **Failure Mental Traps vs. Practical Solutions** two-column comparison table **immediately at the beginning** (right after the metadata and a brief executive hook).  
> 
> A reader should grasp the entire strategic pivot and diagnostic takeaways in under 60 seconds without having to scroll through pages of backstory. Detailed breakdowns, scripts, workflows, and case studies follow afterward.

---

## Crafting High-Impact Mental Traps & Solutions

### 1. Naming the Mental Trap
- **Name the cognitive delusion, not just a generic error.**
  - *Weak:* "Poor planning."
  - *Strong:* "The Exposure Fallacy (Banking on serendipity and broad foot traffic)."
- **Highlight the immediate symptom / consequence:**
  - *Example:* "Spending $10k+ on a booth assuming inbound visitors will produce qualified deals, leading to a calendar of unqualified swag hunters."

### 2. Formulating the Solution
- **State operational behaviors with constraints and numbers, not generic advice.**
  - *Weak:* "Plan ahead and reach out early."
  - *Strong:* "The 3–4 Week Golden Window: Launch 1-on-1 personalized outreach the moment attendee directories drop, before travel agendas lock."
- **Focus on the mechanism:** Explain *how* the solution bypasses the trap (e.g., using personal rep sequencing rather than marketing blasts).

---

## Validation Checklist

Before finalizing a lesson document created with this format:
- [ ] **Table Location:** Is the two-column comparison table positioned within the first screen (before Section 1)?
- [ ] **Two Columns Only:** Does the table strictly adhere to the two columns: `Failure Mental Trap` and `Practical Solution`?
- [ ] **Self-Contained Value:** Can an executive read only the two-column table and immediately know what to stop doing and what to start doing?
- [ ] **No Table Redundancy:** Do the subsequent detailed sections provide depth, evidence, and scripts rather than merely restating the table verbatim?
