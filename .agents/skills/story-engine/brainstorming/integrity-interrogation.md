# Capability: Story Integrity Interrogation

> **When to use**: Always — run before any drafting begins. A technically perfect draft on an unearned story will feel hollow.

---

## Overview

This capability asks whether your story earns the right to be told.

A technically perfect story can still feel hollow. It can have a sharp hook, active voice, and a memorable ending — and leave the reader unsatisfied. Usually the reason is not craft. It is that the protagonist never paid a real price, the storyteller never risked anything true, or the story existed to impress rather than to help someone recognize themselves.

**This runs before craft begins.**

---

## The Four Core Tensions

Every captivating story must navigate at least one of these with genuine difficulty:

> **What truth am I resisting?**
> **Who might recognize themselves in it?**
> **What must change because it is told?**
> **Who might pay the price for my telling it?**

If a story cannot answer any of these with some difficulty, it is not yet ready to be told.

---

## The Interrogation

Run these questions before any drafting. They are not a checklist — they are tensions to sit inside. Not all have clean answers.

### On Honesty

- What truth am I afraid to say — and is that discomfort precisely what the story needs?
- What am I protecting: the truth of the story, or my image as its teller?
- Which detail feels most dangerous to include? Why?
- Where am I hiding behind facts, explanation, or carefully chosen language?
- What is the difference between what I am saying and what I actually mean?

---

### On Recognition

*Apply whether writing from personal experience, constructing a fictional character, or both.*

- What does my listener feel but struggle to put into words?
- What does this story awaken — fear, wonder, grief, joy, courage, belonging, curiosity — and am I allowing it to do that fully?
- Can the listener find themselves here, or can they only observe from a distance?
- Am I making them admire the storyteller, or helping them understand something about themselves?
- If they forget every event, what should remain?

---

### On Stakes & Necessity

- Why must this story be told now?
- Why am I the one who must tell it?
- What did this experience cost — the person living it, the narrator recounting it, or the character inside it?
- What comfortable belief must be surrendered before anything can change?
- Where is the exact moment when a different choice was possible?

> **Note on personal vs. constructed narrative:**
> *"What did this cost me?"* applies to memoir and personal experience.
> When writing fiction or narrative about others, shift the question:
> *"What does it cost the character to remain who they are?"*

---

### On Friction & Transformation

A story where the protagonist moves from flaw to insight without real loss is a parable, not a story. These questions test whether transformation is earned.

- What does the protagonist have to **give up** — not discover — to change?
- Is there a moment where the right action could still fail, and the protagonist knows it?
- Does the character receive a reward too quickly after doing the right thing?
- What would it look like if the protagonist does the right thing and nothing is resolved — could the story hold that?
- Is the transformation declared at the end, or still in tension when the story closes?
- What does the protagonist almost do — and what does that near-choice reveal about who they really are?
- After the climax, is there anything the character must carry that cannot be put down?

---

### On Craft Integrity

- Which specific scene **proves** the truth I am trying to express — not illustrates it, proves it?
- Does the ending explain what the story already showed?
- Does the final line transform how the reader understands the beginning — or does it merely conclude?
- Does this story need stillness, atmosphere, or digression anywhere — and am I allowing that, or forcing momentum where silence would be stronger?
- What am I tempted to cut because it feels too raw — and should I protect it instead?

---

### On Responsibility

- Is this my story to tell?
- Who might pay the price for my vulnerability besides me?
- Am I representing another person's experience truthfully and with care?
- Can I tell the emotional truth without removing someone else's dignity?
- What is the difference between honesty and exposure?

---

## The Impress vs. Recognize Test

Before writing a single word, answer this:

> **Am I writing this to impress people — or to help them recognize something true within themselves?**

If the honest answer is *impress*, do not start drafting. Rewrite the motive, not the story.

---

## Emotional Recognition Research Step

The storyteller's sensing of pain is always partial — shaped by their context, culture, and blind spots. Before finalizing the assessment, search the internet to discover how this wound actually lives in the world beyond the storyteller's experience.

**What to search for:**

1. **Similar stories** — books, films, essays, viral posts, or public narratives that explore the same core wound. Note what emotional language they use in titles, openings, and key lines.
2. **Real expressions of the wound** — Reddit threads, Quora questions, comment sections, forum posts where real people describe this exact pain in their own unpolished words. This is the vocabulary the reader already uses internally.
3. **Cultural and cross-platform resonance** — how has this wound appeared across different cultures or communities? Does it hit differently in different contexts?
4. **What gets shared vs. what gets read** — if similar stories exist, which versions spread widely and which were ignored? What did the widely shared versions name differently?

**Research Output Format:**

```
EMOTIONAL RECOGNITION RESEARCH
--------------------------------
Core wound identified:      [one sentence — the honest, uncomfortable truth]

Similar stories found:
  - [Title / Source]: [what emotional language they used, what they named]
  - [Title / Source]: [what emotional language they used, what they named]

How real people express this wound:
  - "[direct quote from real person — Reddit, comment, forum, etc.]"
  - "[direct quote from real person]"

Emotional vocabulary the reader already uses:
  [list of specific words, phrases, or metaphors that appear repeatedly]

Gaps the storyteller's version may be missing:
  [what angle of this wound appears in the research that the current story does not address?]

Recognition confidence:      [low / medium / high — how broadly does this wound resonate?]
```

Present this research to the user before producing the Story Readiness Assessment.

## Story Readiness Assessment (Output)

After running the interrogation, produce this assessment before handing off to story-craft.

```
STORY READINESS ASSESSMENT
---------------------------
Truth being resisted:   [what is the honest, uncomfortable core?]
Who recognizes this:    [one specific person — their situation, not demographics]
What must change:       [in the reader, not just in the story]
Who pays the price:     [storyteller / character / third party — name them]

Friction check:
  What does the protagonist lose?      [not discover — lose]
  Transformation declared or earned?   [earned / declared / unclear]
  Reward timing:                       [too fast / appropriate / withheld]

Scene proof:            [the one scene that proves the truth — not illustrates it]
Dangerous detail:       [the detail most tempting to remove — should it stay?]
Responsibility flag:    [any ethical concern about who else this story involves]

Readiness verdict:
  [ ] Ready to hand off to story-craft
  [ ] Needs more friction — transformation not earned
  [ ] Needs honesty — storyteller is hiding something
  [ ] Needs necessity — unclear why this must be told now
  [ ] Needs responsibility review — someone else may pay the price
```

## The Reflection Loop

The interrogation is not a single pass. It is an iterative loop.

A first-pass assessment often surfaces a **plausible conflict** — one that feels true enough to write toward. But plausible is not the same as root. The story may be resting on a symptom that points toward a deeper wound it hasn't yet named.

When this happens, do not refine the draft. Reframe the premise.

### The Loop

```
┌─────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│  1. Run interrogation                       │
│  2. Produce Story Readiness Assessment      │
│  3. Present to user — ask:                  │
│     "Does this feel like the real wound,    │
│      or the surface behavior above it?"     │
└───────────────────┬─────────────────────────┘
                    │
        ┌───────────┴──────────┐
        │                      │
   Yes — root found       No — still surface
        │                      │
        ▼                      ▼
   Proceed to            Dig one level deeper:
   style selection       - What fear sits
                           behind this behavior?
                         - What would the person
                           need to believe to act
                           this way?
                         - Reframe the conflict.
                         - Re-run assessment.
                         - Loop again.
```

### What changes in each loop

Each revision pass may change any of these — not just the draft:

- **The conflict itself** — the first named conflict may be a symptom of a deeper one
- **The protagonist's flaw** — what looks like a bad habit may be a rational response to an unexamined fear
- **The metaphor or analogy** — once the real conflict shifts, the supporting image must shift with it
- **The story's emotional target** — what the reader should feel at the end may change entirely

### Rules

- Do not move to craft until the user confirms the conflict feels like the root, not a surface behavior.
- Each loop pass should produce a new Story Readiness Assessment — not a patch on the old one.
- Track which version you are on. If a story has been through 3+ loops, document what shifted between each pass before proceeding.

### Version Log Format

If a story has gone through multiple loops, produce a brief version log before handing off to craft:

```
VERSION LOG
-----------
v0.1 conflict:   [what the story was originally about]
v0.1 problem:    [why it felt surface or incomplete]

v0.2 conflict:   [the deeper conflict uncovered in loop 2]
v0.2 change:     [what shifted — premise / metaphor / flaw / emotional target]

[continue for each loop]

Final conflict:  [the version the user confirmed feels true]
```

## Common Failure Modes

| Failure | What It Looks Like | Question to Ask |
|:---|:---|:---|
| Parable, not story | Protagonist gains insight without losing anything | What does the character give up? |
| Too-fast reward | Character does the right thing; universe immediately validates them | Does the story hold ambiguity after the right action? |
| Performative vulnerability | Storyteller shares something personal but avoids the most dangerous truth | Which detail am I most afraid to include? |
| Writing to impress | Clever language, but reader cannot find themselves in it | Am I helping them recognize, or making them admire? |
| Explained ending | Final lines state the lesson the story already proved | Does the last line earn silence, or break it? |
| Collateral exposure | Story is honest about the teller but careless about others in it | Who else appears here, and are they protected? |
