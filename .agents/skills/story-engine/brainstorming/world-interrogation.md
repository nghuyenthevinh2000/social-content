# Capability: Story World Interrogation

> **When to use:** Independently, when developing a topic into a consistent story world or diagnosing an existing draft. Start directly with the topic and emotions; complete this method's own world brief and approval gate.

## Purpose

Help the writer identify what they want to explore, what emotions they want to express and evoke, and which world can honestly contain the story. Then interrogate the details until the world's law, primitive symbols, relationships, exclusions, and ending form a complete, consistent architecture.

**A world is selected by its governing rule, not by its mood.** Sadness does not automatically mean Suffer World. Peace does not automatically mean Void World. Hard work does not automatically mean Forge World. A world must govern what actions accomplish, what time changes, and what the ending allows.

Use [the worldmaking framework](world/worldmaking-framework.md) for structural questions. Read the selected world's full definition before conducting its symbol interview. Those files are the source of truth; the questions below are interview aids.

**Standalone scope:** This method requires no other brainstorming method, integrity assessment, emotional recognition research, or platform-selection workflow. Its inputs are the writer's topic, intended emotions, and available material; its output is an approved Story World Brief. The world definitions are reference material, not additional interviews. Any later writing process is chosen separately by the user.

## Interview Rules

1. **Ask in the user's language.** Use ordinary words first; introduce world terminology through their answers.
2. **Ask two to four focused questions per round.** Start with topic and emotion, then prioritize the largest gap or contradiction. Do not present every question at once.
3. **Extract answers already provided.** Read supplied notes or drafts. Summarize what they establish, mark interpretations as provisional, and ask only what remains unresolved.
4. **Distinguish three emotional layers:** what the person felt then, what the narrator feels now, and what the reader should feel. These can differ.
5. **Keep evidence separate from intention.** Label details as observed, remembered, reported, sourced, proposed, or fictional. A desired emotion is not evidence that an event occurred.
6. **Do not manufacture missing symbols.** In nonfiction, ask for actual details. If none exist, reconsider the scope or world. In fiction, offer possibilities clearly labeled as proposals and ask the user to choose.
7. **Complete all eight symbol records.** Each must have a concrete role, manifestation, relationship, and status. Not every primitive needs its own scene, but none may disappear from the architecture without explanation.
8. **Absence can be meaningful, but is not an automatic pass.** Ask what the missing symbol's absence does, where its consequence is visible, and whether the selected world's system can still hold. An unsupported absence remains a gap.
9. **Do not force the material into the recommended world.** If the facts and preferred emotional ending conflict, explain the conflict and let the writer revise the intention, select another world, or establish an explicit crossing.
10. **Do not draft during interrogation.** Produce interview notes and a world brief. This method is complete when its own completion gate is satisfied and the user approves the brief. Drafting is a separate next step requested by the user.

## Round 1 — Topic, Experience, and Emotion

Start with questions such as:

- What do you want to write about, and what particular experience, event, or question makes it matter to you?
- Is this personal experience, someone else's account, fiction, or an essay/report? Who or what do we follow?
- What emotions do you want to express? Which did you actually feel at the time, and which arose later?
- What should the reader feel at the beginning, during the central moment, and after the final line?

Follow up on broad labels. If the user says “sad,” ask whether it is grief, humiliation, helplessness, loneliness, disappointment, or something else. Ask for the situation, bodily sensation, gesture, thought, or words that distinguish it. Do not infer a hidden wound where the writer describes curiosity, joy, or recognition.

Record an **emotional trajectory**, not just a list:

```text
Topic / central question:
Form and factual basis:
Person or system followed:
Emotion in the experience:
Narrator's emotion now:
Reader: opening → central moment → ending
Detail grounding each emotional change:
What must remain unresolved:
```

## Round 2 — Discover the Governing Law

Ask what the story actually permits, rather than asking the writer to pick a philosophical label:

- What is threatened, missing, or falsely promised at the beginning?
- What does someone do, and what changes because of it? If nothing changes, what persists?
- What makes that action right within this story: renewed life, honest enclosure, agency, relinquishment, or a fitting response?
- What does the ending restore, build, release, correct, or leave unavailable?
- What must the story never promise the reader?

Use this routing table provisionally:

| World | Exact truth standard | Investigate when… |
| :------ | :--------------------- | :------------------ |
| [Happy](world/happy-world.md) | A thing is right if it increases the capacity of a being to be alive. | Connection, joy, rest, meaning, or another living capacity is restored or sustained. |
| [Suffer](world/suffer-world.md) | A thing is right if it does not offer a way out. | Effort, witness, and time do not breach the enclosure or convert suffering into growth. |
| [Forge](world/forge-world.md) | A thing is right if it asserts agency against the void. | Deeds meet resistance, incur cost, and carve form or sovereignty from unformed ground. |
| [Void](world/void-world.md) | A thing is right if it dissolves distinction and returns to zero. | The movement is toward unburdening and dissolution, rather than renewed striving or permanent achievement. |
| [Zhong](world/zhong-world.md) | A thing is right if it meets the actual circumstance in due measure while preserving the capacity to respond again. | Attention, limits, and feedback replace a rigid response with proportionate judgment. |

### Present a World Fit Assessment

```text
WORLD FIT ASSESSMENT — v[number]
Recommended governing world:
Its exact law:
Evidence from the user's material:
How the emotional trajectory fits:
Closest alternative and the decisive difference:
Supporting symbols from other worlds, if any:
Contradiction or uncertainty still open:
```

Ask: **“Does this rule describe what you want the story to make true? Is the emotional ending also right for you?”**

Wait for confirmation before the full symbol interview. If the writer prefers another world, test its rule against the same evidence. If none fits, leave the selection unresolved and ask which governing assumption needs to change; do not invent a sixth world without the user's agreement.

## Round 3 — Complete the Selected World's Symbols

Read the full selected world file. Create a ledger with every one of its eight primitives, using the names and definitions in that file. For each primitive, recover:

1. **Manifestation:** the action, object, setting, sensation, exchange, or condition that embodies it.
2. **Specifics:** who, where, when, what happened, and what was perceived or said. Exact dialogue is required only when genuinely available; label paraphrases.
3. **Function:** what it enables, compounds, dissolves, or corrects in this particular story.
4. **Relationship:** which other primitive it affects, and the concrete link between them.
5. **Shadow or pressure:** how its absence or inversion is shown, or what keeps it from functioning.
6. **Evidence and status:** where the detail came from and what remains unanswered.

Use **grounded**, **partial**, **missing**, **contradictory**, or **resolved absence** as statuses. “Resolved absence” requires an explicit explanation consistent with the world definition and confirmed by the user. It cannot replace a primitive indispensable to the intended arc.

### Happy World Questions

| Primitive | Targeted question |
| :---------- | :------------------ |
| Joy | Where does being alive become desirable again, beyond receiving a reward? What detail shows it? |
| Connection | Who or what genuinely reaches the person? What exchange shows that distance changing? |
| Meaning | What action matters beyond its immediate completion, and to whom? |
| Play | Where is there exploration without a required result? If none, what is constricting that freedom? |
| Rest | What renews capacity rather than merely stopping activity? What becomes possible afterward? |
| Gift | What is freely given or received, and what does that exchange create? |
| Growth | What possibility becomes available that was previously outside the person's reach? |
| Beauty | What is perceived fully rather than merely used? How does it restore presence? |

### Suffer World Questions

| Primitive | Targeted question |
| :---------- | :------------------ |
| Weight | What ordinary act has become persistently harder? How does the body carry that pressure? |
| Futility | What effort repeats without accumulation? What shows that another strategy is not a hidden exit? |
| Isolation | Who is present but cannot reach the person? How is the distance demonstrated? |
| Silence | What cannot be carried by the available words? What is said or withheld instead? |
| Loss | What absence has permanently reorganized the person's world? What routine passes through it? |
| The Inescapability of Consciousness | What knowledge keeps returning, even during distraction or apparent respite? |
| Indifference | What continues unaffected while this suffering occurs? Where is response unavailable? |
| Erosion | What desire, imagined future, or inner capacity has diminished over time? |

These are questions about the chosen narrative architecture, not claims that real suffering has no possible help or recovery. Do not suppress an actual recovery to preserve this classification; change the classification or mark a crossing.

### Forge World Questions

| Primitive | Targeted question |
| :---------- | :------------------ |
| The Abyss | What unformed ground or collapse of inherited meaning leaves the person without a ready-made answer? |
| The Spark | What first refusal or decision turns that condition into a reason to act? |
| Resistance | What pushes back? Describe an actual failed attempt or stubborn constraint. |
| The Strike | Which concrete deed creates consequences beyond planning or intention? |
| The Cost | What finite resource, comfort, opportunity, or belief is actually spent? |
| The Form | What has been made that can now bear weight? What evidence distinguishes it from an aspiration? |
| Sovereignty | What can the person now own, decide, or answer for without borrowed validation? |
| Resonance | Who recognizes or joins the work through shared craft or cost? What makes that recognition substantive? |

### Void World Questions

| Primitive | Targeted question |
| :---------- | :------------------ |
| The Zero | What baseline remains when the disturbance settles? How is it experienced without deprivation or terror? |
| The Mirage | Which form, status, or identity is initially treated as substantial or lasting? |
| Dissolution | How does its grip loosen, and what concrete detail shows the unmaking? |
| Stillness | Where does striving settle rather than freeze in fear? |
| The Hollow | What reveals the absence of the fixed core the person expected to find? |
| The Unnamed | What no longer needs classification or explanation? How is that different from being unable to speak? |
| The Leveling | Which hierarchy or distinction loses its claim to ultimate importance? |
| Cessation | What demand or struggle finally ceases, without being converted into a new achievement? |

Do not equate illness, involuntary loss of speech, despair, or self-destruction with this world's unburdening. Ask what the experience actually was before assigning a symbol.

### Zhong World Questions

| Primitive | Targeted question |
| :---------- | :------------------ |
| Attention | What observed fact interrupts the person's preferred explanation? |
| The Center | What commitment or orientation remains trustworthy while the method changes? |
| Measure | What is enough for this actual need? What establishes excess and deficiency here? |
| The Boundary | What must be refused or limited, and whom does that limit protect? |
| Timing | Why does the moment, duration, or sequence matter to the response? |
| Yielding | What method, certainty, or unnecessary force is relinquished? What purpose remains? |
| Return | What consequence comes back as evidence and changes the next judgment? |
| Reciprocity | Who else bears the burden or holds a legitimate claim? How does the adjustment account for them? |

## Round 4 — Test the World's Physics

After the symbol ledger is populated, ask the questions that expose structural contradictions:

- **Relationships:** Can we trace the primitive interactions through actual events or conditions, rather than a list of themes?
- **Time:** Does time generate capacity, erode the person, accumulate tested form, dissolve distinctions, or permit correction? What demonstrates that temporal logic?
- **Exclusions:** Which temptations in this story would violate the selected world's full exclusion list?
- **Agency and consequence:** Does the person's action produce the kind of result the world permits? Are intention and observed outcome being confused?
- **Ending:** Does the final state obey the same law as the beginning and central moment? What remains unresolved or irreversible?
- **Emotional honesty:** Does the desired reader feeling arise from the supplied details, or would it require an invented rescue, cost, realization, or reward?

For an essay or report, use evidence, reasoning, and recommendations where a scene would not fit the form. A proposed solution is not an observed recovery. Do not turn a report into a protagonist-led story merely to satisfy the ledger.

### Crossings and Supporting Symbols

A borrowed image does not change the governing world. A change in the truth standard does.

If the writer wants an intentional crossing, specify the starting and ending worlds, the exact transition, its trigger, its cost or relinquishment, and the governing law on each side. Complete separate ledgers for each world used as a governing system; audit each segment and the transition. Do not describe an unexplained mixture as a complete single world.

For example, a bleak opening followed by recovery is not automatically a Suffer → Happy crossing. First ask whether enclosure was ever truly structural, or whether the story was Happy World with a threatened capacity throughout.

## The Missing-Symbol Loop

Continue until the world is complete with grounded details and the user confirms it:

```text
1. Summarize established details and update the ledger.
2. Name the highest-impact missing, partial, or contradictory item.
3. Explain briefly why that gap affects the world's law or emotional arc.
4. Ask 2–4 concrete follow-up questions about that gap.
5. Wait for answers; do not fill the gap with invented facts.
6. Recheck the symbol, its relationships, and the ending.
7. Repeat until no unresolved gaps or contradictions remain.
```

Do not repeat an answered question. If an answer remains abstract, ask for one instance: **“When did that happen? What did you do or notice? What changed afterward?”**

If the writer cannot supply a detail, ask whether to retrieve evidence, narrow the piece, accept a justified structural absence, or reconsider the world. “I don't know” remains unresolved unless one of those choices actually resolves it. If the user pauses the process, retain a **Not ready** verdict and record the open questions.

### Completion Gate

- [ ] Topic, form, factual basis, and emotional trajectory are confirmed.
- [ ] Governing world and its exact law are confirmed by the user.
- [ ] All eight primitive records are grounded or have justified, confirmed absences consistent with the world.
- [ ] No indispensable primitive is absent; no partial, missing, or contradictory records remain.
- [ ] Symbol relationships, temporal logic, operations, and exclusions are explicit.
- [ ] Beginning, central movement, and ending obey the governing law, or an explicit crossing is approved.
- [ ] The reader test matches the intended emotional ending without unsupported promises.
- [ ] The complete world brief is explicitly approved by the user.

## Independent Output and Draft Consistency

Deliver the approved world brief as a self-contained output. Do not automatically launch another brainstorming method or require a platform-specific brief. If the user later requests a draft or review, use this world brief as its consistency reference, regardless of the writing method selected.

Before presenting the draft, audit it against the brief:

1. Does each planned symbol still have its concrete manifestation or meaningful structural absence?
2. Do actions and consequences obey the selected law and temporal logic?
3. Has a forbidden rescue, reward, moral midpoint, permanent form, or compensating ledger entered the story?
4. Does the ending fulfill the emotional contract and reader test through details rather than unsupported declaration?
5. Are newly added nonfiction claims grounded in the writer's account or identified sources?

If the draft exposes missing information or changes the world, reopen the relevant questions. A change to the governing law, symbolic architecture, or emotional destination requires an updated world brief and renewed user approval before redrafting.
