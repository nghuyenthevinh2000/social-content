## Core Principle: Connection Beats Information

> *"People don't share what they learn. They share what they feel."*

Every format, rule, and technique in this file is in service of one belief:

**Connection beats information.**

Facts are forgettable. Feelings are not. A post that makes someone feel seen, called out, relieved, or understood will outperform a post that is merely accurate or comprehensive — every time.

This means:
- **Don't lead with data.** Lead with the human moment behind the data.
- **Don't explain what you know.** Show what you've lived.
- **Don't write to inform.** Write to make one person feel less alone in something they already privately believe.

Every format below is a different vehicle for the same destination: making the reader feel something real.

## Platform Conventions for LinkedIn

Before choosing a format, understand the environment:

- **The "see more" cutoff** happens after ~3 lines of text. Your hook must work before that break.
- **White space is mandatory** — LinkedIn readers scroll fast on mobile. Short lines, paragraph breaks every 1-3 sentences.
- **Text-only posts often outperform image posts** — algorithm rewards dwell time and comments.
- **Ideal post length**: fits comfortably on a phone screen. If it doesn't, it's probably too long.
- **Hashtags**: 3-5 max, placed at the end, not mid-sentence.
- **Emojis**: Use sparingly to mark list items or section breaks. Never to substitute emotion.
- **No "LinkedIn family"** — it marks you as someone who writes to no one.

## Format 1: Hook + List (Authority / Education)

**When to use**: Teaching a specific lesson, sharing a breakdown, positioning expertise.
**Engagement driver**: List structure promises a complete, scannable payoff.

### Structure

```
[HOOK — one line. Bold claim, quoted objection, or insider signal.]
[REHOOK — one or two lines. Context, promise, or why this matters now.]

[Transition line — "Here's what most miss:" / "The breakdown:"]

1. [Point — grounded in scene or specific, not vague]
2. [Point]
3. [Point]
4. [Point — can go up to 5-7 max before it feels bloated]

[Single closing line — gut-punch, recontextualization, or image. Not a summary.]
```

### Rules
- Write the hook **after** the rest of the post
- Each list item = one specific, standalone insight (not a category label)
- No item should be a vague adjective — name the scene or the result
- Closing line must not restate the list — it should add a new dimension

### Example skeleton

```
"Can't one person just handle all of marketing?" …said by someone who's clearly never done it.

Marketing isn't one job. It's a mashup of 13 different roles — and most companies hire for one.

Here's what you're actually getting when you hire "a marketer":

1. Brand strategist (who you are)
2. Content creator (what you say)
3. SEO specialist (how they find you)
4. Paid ads manager (how you reach them fast)
5. Email marketer (how you keep them)
6. Analytics lead (whether it's working)
7. Community manager (what they say back)

Hire one person for all of this.

Then wonder why the results are underwhelming.
```

## Format 2: Contrarian / Myth-Buster

**When to use**: Establishing authority by taking a position. Generating comments and debate.
**Engagement driver**: Cognitive dissonance — readers feel compelled to agree or push back.

### Structure

```
[HOOK — state the false belief as others hold it, or directly challenge it]
[REHOOK — acknowledge why people believe this, then break the frame]

[The truth — specific, not vague. Name what's actually happening.]

[Why the common belief persists — show you understand the pull of the wrong answer]

[The implication — what this means for the reader's behavior]

[Closing — leave with the uncomfortable truth, not a reassuring wrap-up]
```

### Rules
- Don't be contrarian for shock value — be contrarian because you have the receipts
- Name the specific belief you're dismantling (don't say "some people think")
- Give the reader an alternative they can actually act on
- Closing should feel like a door that opens, not a door that closes

### Example skeleton

```
Posting every day is not a LinkedIn strategy.

It's a content treadmill dressed up as consistency.

Here's what most people don't realize:

The algorithm doesn't reward frequency.
It rewards relevance, dwell time, and comments.

You can post 30 times a month and be invisible.
You can post 8 times a month and be everywhere.

The difference?
One post is built around a specific person's specific pain.
The other is built around the creator's posting schedule.

Post less. Mean more.
```

## Format 3: Scene → Insight (Personal Story)

**When to use**: Building trust, humanizing expertise, making a lesson memorable through lived experience.
**Engagement driver**: Emotional identification — readers find themselves in the scene before the insight lands.

### Structure

```
[HOOK — open in the scene. No context. No "Today I want to share." Active, specific, present-tense feel.]
[REHOOK — one line of tension or what was at stake]

[Scene — show, don't tell. 2-4 sentences that paint the moment.]

[The turning point — what changed, what was said, what was seen]

[The lesson extracted — "Here's what that moment taught me:" / "I've thought about that ever since."]

[Transferable insight — what the reader can do with this]

[Closing — holds the emotional weight. Often one short line.]
```

### Rules
- **No context before conflict** — do not start by explaining the backstory
- **Show the scene** — physical, specific, active voice
- Lesson must be **transferable** — the reader should see themselves, not just admire you
- Apply the **"Dear Son" technique** while drafting (write it as a letter to someone you love, delete before publishing)
- Write the hook last

### Example skeleton

```
My first client called me at 7am to say they were ending the contract.

I had worked 90 days on their brand.
Written 40 posts.
Built a system they said they loved.

They had one comment:
"It doesn't sound like us."

I asked one question I should have asked on day one:
"What does 'us' sound like?"

Silence.

They didn't know.
And I had never asked.

Since then, I start every engagement with a voice document.
Not a brief.
Not a questionnaire.
A recording of them talking — unscripted — about why they do what they do.

You can't write a brand voice.
You can only find it.
```

## Format 4: The Insider / Rumour

**When to use**: Timely content — algorithm changes, industry shifts, observations from inside a space.
**Engagement driver**: FOMO + authority. Readers feel they're getting something most people don't have.

### Structure

```
[HOOK — casual, observational. Implies exclusive or early knowledge without claiming it explicitly.]
[REHOOK — the specific thing worth knowing, stated precisely]

[Body — what changed / what was observed / what the data shows]

[What this means for the reader — "What this means for your content:" / "Here's the practical implication:"]

[1-3 specific action points]

[Closing — direct, no fluff]
```

### Rules
- Hook must feel **overheard**, not announced — "I wasn't shocked… but still had to share this."
- Be specific in the body — vague observations aren't insider knowledge
- The implication section is **the most important part** — this is where the reader's behavior changes
- Never hype what you're about to say. Just say it.

### Example skeleton

```
LinkedIn quietly changed something last week.
Most people haven't noticed.

Text-only posts with 3-line formatting are now getting 40% more reach than the same posts with images.

I've tested this across 12 client accounts over 30 days.
Consistent across industries.

What this means for your content:

1. Strip the stock photo from your next post.
2. Tighten the hook to 3 lines or fewer.
3. Add one line of white space after every 1-2 sentences.

Algorithm favors what keeps people reading.
Not what looks impressive in a feed.
```

## Format 5: The Proof Point (Authority Without Selling)

**When to use**: Business development. Making inbound happen without writing sales posts.
**Engagement driver**: Specificity + credibility. Readers trust results they can picture.

### Structure

```
[HOOK — a specific result, not a claim. Numbers, timelines, names of what happened.]
[REHOOK — why this is worth understanding (not bragging — transferable insight ahead)]

[What you did instead of the obvious thing]

[The principle behind it — transferable, not personal]

[Invitation — open question or statement that draws prospects to you without asking for them]

[Closing — one line. Often a restatement of the core principle in a new frame.]
```

### Rules
- Lead with **result**, not with effort (don't say "I worked hard" — say "6 months of clients booked")
- Make the **principle** transferable — readers should be able to apply it without hiring you
- The **invitation** is implicit — a question that self-selects for people who want more
- Never use "DM me if interested" — let the proof do the work

### Example skeleton

```
6 months of clients booked.
Zero sales posts written.

Here's what I did instead:

Every week, I wrote one post that proved I understand one specific problem my clients have.
Not solved it for them.
Not offered a service.
Just proved I've thought about it longer and more specifically than anyone else.

The principle:
People don't hire consultants who say they can help.
They hire consultants who already understand the problem
before anyone's booked a call.

If someone reads 8 of your posts and doesn't think "this person gets me," 
your content isn't working.

Mine did.
```

## Pre-Publish Checklist (LinkedIn-Specific)

Before posting, run through:

- [ ] Does the first line work **before** "see more"?
- [ ] Is there a rehook immediately after the hook?
- [ ] Is the post one idea — not two or three?
- [ ] Is there enough white space? (test on mobile view)
- [ ] Does every list item contain something **specific**, not vague?
- [ ] Is the last line a **punch**, not a summary?
- [ ] Does it sound like a human, not a brand?
- [ ] Is this **helpful** to one specific person — not interesting to a crowd?
- [ ] Did you write the hook **last**?

## Common LinkedIn Failure Modes

| Failure | Signal | Fix |
|:--------|:-------|:----|
| Opening with context | "Today I want to share…" | Open in the scene or with the claim |
| Writing to everyone | "Hey LinkedIn family!" | Address one specific person's specific pain |
| Leading with a stat | "78% of people say…" | Lead with the human moment behind the stat |
| Explained ending | Final line restates the lesson | End on an image, question, or gut-punch |
| Passive constructions | "The team was led by…" | Flip every "was [verb]ed" |
| Image for image's sake | Stock photo that adds nothing | Go text-only; add image only if it proves something |
| Posting without rehook | Hook followed by body directly | Always write line 2 as a door-slammer |
