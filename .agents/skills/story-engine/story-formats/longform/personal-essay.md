# Personal Essay — Long-Form Craft

Use for personal essays, reflective blog posts, and narrative newsletters. Apply after the Story Engine brief is approved.

## Flexible Shape

**Entry → Development → Turn → Landing**

- **Entry:** Give the reader something specific to care about: a moment, tension, observation, or unresolved question. Supply enough context to orient them.
- **Development:** Select the events and details that deepen that interest. Weave in reflection where it helps the reader understand why something matters.
- **Turn:** Let an event, consequence, or shift in perspective change how the reader understands what came before. This can emerge gradually.
- **Landing:** Leave the reader with an image, action, admission, or precise insight. Stop when the meaning lands.

These are narrative functions, not required headings or equal-length sections. Combine, reorder, or revisit them to fit the story.

## Write with Clarity

- **Keep one thread.** Each paragraph should advance the experience or deepen its meaning. Cut side paths that do neither.
- **Make the sequence legible.** Identify people, mark meaningful time shifts, and distinguish what you knew then from what you understand now.
- **Connect events.** Show why a choice or consequence followed; chronology alone does not explain causality.
- **Use concrete language.** Prefer exact nouns, direct verbs, and familiar words. Keep imagery only when it makes the experience clearer.
- **Interpret when needed.** Scenes make experience tangible; reflection makes its significance understandable. Avoid explaining the same point twice.

## Keep the Reader Engaged

- **Create honest curiosity.** Open a question the story can explore. Avoid exaggerated stakes or withholding basic facts for suspense.
- **Vary the pace.** Slow down for consequential moments; summarize transitions and routine stretches. Let quiet moments breathe.
- **Choose revealing details.** Keep the gesture, object, or line that exposes a relationship or feeling. Do not invent details for effect.
- **Let paragraphs breathe.** Vary sentence length and break paragraphs when the action, thought, or emphasis shifts. Use section breaks for substantial changes in time or focus.
- **Earn the ending.** A lesson may be stated plainly if the experience supports it. Avoid a repeated summary, forced resolution, or automatic call to action.

## Final Pass

Can the reader follow what happened, feel why it mattered, and understand what changed—or remains unresolved? Cut anything that makes those harder.
