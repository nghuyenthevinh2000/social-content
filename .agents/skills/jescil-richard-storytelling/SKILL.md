# Jescil Richard Storytelling Framework

## Overview

Connection beats information. Simple words with emotional truth outlast complex words with facts. Every piece of writing is a private conversation with one person.

## The 10 Principles

### 1. Start with a Bang
First sentence = make readers curious, shocked, or intrigued. Give them no choice but to read on.

```
❌ "Today I want to share some thoughts about failure."
✅ "The day I got fired was the best day of my life."
```

### 2. Show, Don't Tell
Paint pictures. Replace state-of-being with scene.

```
❌ "I was surprised."
✅ "My jaw dropped when I opened the package."
```

### 3. Emotions Over Facts
People forget statistics. They remember how something made them feel.
Target: laugh, cry, belong, or reflect.

### 4. Write to ONE Person
Even if a million read it — write as if it's a private conversation with one specific human. Use "you", not "you all".

### 5. Have a Story Goal
Before writing, answer: **What do I want my reader to FEEL when they finish this?**
Clarity here changes everything.

### 6. Active Voice
"She broke the glass" > "The glass was broken by her."
Sharp. Direct. Powerful.

### 7. K.I.S.S. — Keep It Simple, Storyteller
Complexity kills connection. Simple words, deep meaning. That's the magic.

### 8. End with a Punch
Last line should linger. Make it memorable. Make it matter.

```
❌ "So that's my story about persistence."
✅ "The only way out was through — and I walked out the other side."
```

### 9. Write in a Conducive Environment
Distracted storyteller = distracted reader. Remove noise before you write.

### 10. No Distractions While Writing
One tab. One focus. Get in the zone before the first word.

## Quick Reference

| Principle | Core Question |
|-----------|--------------|
| Bang opening | Does line 1 create irresistible curiosity? |
| Show/don't tell | Can I replace an adjective with a scene? |
| Emotion first | What will they FEEL, not just know? |
| One person | Who specifically am I writing to? |
| Story goal | What's the one feeling I'm engineering? |
| Active voice | Is the subject doing the action? |
| KISS | Can I say this in fewer, simpler words? |
| Punch ending | Will the last line stay with them? |

## Parameter Interview (Run First — Every Time)

When this skill is activated, ask the following questions **one at a time** before doing any writing. Do not batch them. Wait for each answer before asking the next.

**Q1 — Platform**
> "What platform is this for?" (LinkedIn / X / Instagram / other)

*Why it matters:* Tone, length, and format differ per platform. LinkedIn tolerates long-form; X punishes it.

---

**Q2 — Audience**
> "Who is the one person you're writing to? Describe them briefly — their role, their pain, what keeps them up at night."

*Why it matters:* Principle 4 (write to ONE person) only works if you've named them.

---

**Q3 — Topic / Raw Story**
> "What's the topic or experience you want to share? Give me the messy version — I'll help shape it."

*Why it matters:* Raw material first. Structure second.

---

**Q4 — Desired Emotion**
> "When someone finishes reading this, what do you want them to FEEL?" (inspired / called out / relieved / motivated / curious / other)

*Why it matters:* Principle 5 (story goal) must be set before the first word.

---

**Q5 — Format**
> "What format works best?" (single hook post / carousel / short thread / caption)

*Why it matters:* Structure of the brief changes by format.

---

Once all 5 answers are collected, produce the **Story Brief** (see below). Do not write the post.

---

## Story Brief (Output After Interview)

Produce a structured brief the user finalizes before writing. Format:

```
STORY BRIEF
-----------
Platform:       [answer]
Writing to:     [one person, described in one line]
Story goal:     [the one emotion to engineer]
Format:         [post type]

Opening bang:   [2-3 candidate first lines — strong, varied, competing]
Core scene:     [the 1-2 moment(s) to show, not tell]
Emotional arc:  [how the reader moves from start to end]
Punch ending:   [2-3 candidate last lines]

Principles to watch:
- [flag any specific risks given this topic/audience, e.g. "avoid leading with stat"]
```

User reviews and approves the brief. Only then move to drafting if asked.

---

## Workflow

1. **Run parameter interview** — 5 questions, one at a time
2. **Produce story brief** — structured output, not a draft
3. **User approves brief** — iterate if needed
4. **Draft on request** — apply all 10 principles to write the post

## Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Fix |
|---------|-----|
| Opening with context/background | Open with conflict, surprise, or tension |
| Writing to "everyone" | Pick one reader, write to them specifically |
| Leading with a stat or fact | Lead with the human moment behind the stat |
| Weak ending (summary) | End on an image, a question, or a gut-punch line |
| Passive constructions | Find every "was [verb]ed" and flip it |
