| Research focus | Direction |
| --- | --- |
| Goal | Understand potential business clients: what they produce, which paper they buy, why they choose a supplier, and what would make them change. |
| Outcome | One accumulating `research.json`, saved in the chosen research directory, rather than a new disconnected report for every session. Start with catalog context and planned keywords; add discoveries and deepen existing records as research progresses. |
| Product reference | [Actual catalog](../local/paper-ink-product/product-catalog.md): Ánh Kim 180, Ivory 230, Nhung đỏ 120. Ink is not listed, so this plan focuses on paper. |
| Starting audience | Wedding-invitation workshops; begin with `thiệp cưới` and `xưởng in thiệp cưới`, then investigate their actual material choices. |
| Twenty-client checkpoint | Study 20 distinct relevant businesses in the starting audience, then summarize shared needs, differences, and unanswered questions. This is an initial understanding of prospects, not proof of demand or 20 paying clients. |
| Expansion | Research packaging and seasonal/traditional-print businesses separately; do not assume invitation-workshop findings describe all three markets. |

| JSON section | Information accumulated | How it helps understand clients |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `context` | Research objective, actual catalog products, starting audience and current focus. | Keeps discoveries connected to the products and audience being studied. |
| `keyword_queue` | Keywords not yet tried, why they are worth searching, and the discussion/profile that suggested them. | Lets client vocabulary guide the next search without confusing planned searches with completed ones. |
| `searches` | Each actual keyword tried, when, where, wide/targeted intent, group scope if any, result links/record IDs, outcome and useful new terms. Preserve repeat searches as separate attempts. | Shows which language and search locations reveal relevant clients, including unsuccessful searches and their limitations. |
| `trade_communities` | Stable ID, URL, name, topic, discovery search IDs, observed business roles, recurring discussion themes, useful post references, and unanswered questions. | Explains where clients gather, what they discuss, and which communities reveal different needs. |
| `profiles` | Stable ID, URL, name, page/person type, discovered-through search/community/post references, and related business ID where known. | Connects who posts with where and how they were discovered; multiple profiles need not mean multiple businesses. |
| `profiles[].details` | Description, location, services, operation/role, customer types, materials explicitly mentioned, and dated follower snapshots when available. | Builds an increasingly useful picture of the business rather than a list of names. |
| `posts` | Stable ID, URL, author/profile reference, community reference where relevant, publication date, relevant text, product/application context, and dated public engagement snapshots. | Shows actual work, sourcing language and current concerns. Include collected-window coverage so missing posts are not mistaken for inactivity. |
| `comments` | Relevant comment/reply text, source URL or platform ID where available, parent post/comment reference, and author reference when known. | Preserves buyer questions, recommendations and production discussions without attributing every comment to the post owner. |
| `profiles[].client_understanding` | Source-backed findings about paper use, purchasing role, needs, pains, buying triggers, supplier choices, buying frequency, seasonality, objections and decision criteria; open questions beside each gap. | Gradually turns a profile into an understanding of how that client operates and buys. |
| `insights` | Cross-business patterns, supporting profile/post/comment IDs, counterexamples, relevant catalog products, and whether observed, inferred or still hypothetical. | Separates a recurring client need from one person's comment or our assumption. |
| `product_approach` | Current audience priorities, relevant paper/application, message/offer direction, supporting insight IDs and remaining uncertainties. Preserve dated revisions when the interpretation changes. | Records how accumulated client understanding changes the proposed approach; initially empty, then provisional at the 20-business checkpoint. |
| `next_questions` | Unanswered client questions and the next keyword, community, profile or conversation likely to resolve each. | Makes the next session deepen understanding rather than restart discovery. |

| Research moment | Update to the same JSON | Accumulation principle |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Before searching | Read existing discoveries and open questions; choose from `keyword_queue` or add a justified new term. | Reuse what is already known. No searches are marked tried before execution. |
| After each keyword attempt | Append a `searches` entry, add/link discovered communities and profiles, and update the queue with useful new terms. | Keep empty, blocked and irrelevant outcomes too; they explain gaps, not absence of demand. |
| After inspecting a community | Enrich its existing record and link relevant posts, authors and business profiles. | Retain the discovery path from keyword to community to client. |
| After inspecting a profile | Fill known details and client findings; attach sources and unresolved questions. | Enrich the same profile rather than create duplicates. Link profiles to one business only when supported, not by similar names alone. |
| After reading posts/comments | Add unseen records, link them to the relevant profiles/communities, and add dated engagement observations when revisiting. | Preserve original text and prior observations; unknown is `null` or an open question, not zero or an invented fact. |
| When learning something new | Add a source-backed finding or revise the interpretation with its date and reason; keep conflicting observations visible. | Observations, self-reports and inferences remain distinguishable. Store only business-relevant material from permitted sources. |
| At 20 distinct relevant businesses | Compare the accumulated client understanding, record recurring patterns and differences in `insights`, and draft `product_approach`. | Count businesses, not posts/accounts; keep conclusions scoped to the audience actually studied. Twenty is a synthesis checkpoint, not proof of demand. |
| At the end of a session | Save the JSON with an updated timestamp, a short summary of new learning and the next unresolved questions. | Save throughout research, not only at the checkpoint; never replace existing research with a partial session result. |

| Research step | How to do it on Facebook | What it helps us understand about clients |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Wide search | Search the broad keywords above in posts, pages and groups where available; also try accentless variants and indexed-web searches such as `site:facebook.com "xưởng in thiệp cưới"`. | Who operates in the market, what they call their work, and where they gather. |
| 2. Find trade communities | Review roughly 3–5 relevant groups initially. Read both popular discussions and recent ordinary questions. | Where business owners exchange advice, seek suppliers, discuss materials and recommend alternatives. |
| 3. Understand who is posting | Read the post and relevant business description; distinguish workshop, designer/reseller, material supplier and finished-product customer. | Whether the person buys raw paper, influences its selection, resells finished products, or serves as a competitor rather than a client. |
| 4. Follow specific needs | Search within relevant communities using paper/application terms plus `tìm nguồn`, `cần mua`, `báo giá`, `khổ giấy`, `định lượng`, `giao gấp`. Expand using the clients' own vocabulary. | What prompts a purchase, which specifications matter, and whether the need is routine, urgent or seasonal. |
| 5. Read business profiles | Inspect descriptions, services, locations and public business information. Use follower counts only as background. | What they sell, whom they serve, whether they produce or outsource, and where they operate. Followers do not establish purchase volume. |
| 6. Review recent work | Read available posts from the last 30 days: product launches, workshop footage, order updates, material discussions and service promotions. For seasonal businesses, also review the previous relevant season where accessible. | Current product mix, production context, customer positioning and seasonal workload. Do not infer exact paper specifications or order volume from photos. |
| 7. Read comments and replies | Inspect discussions under material questions and relevant business posts, not only engagement totals. Separate the business owner's statements from other commenters' needs. | Which questions recur, what buyers struggle with, what customers request, and how businesses explain their choices. |
| 8. Investigate supplier choices | Look for supplier recommendations, sourcing requests, complaints and switching accounts. Ask about the most recent purchase when public discussion leaves gaps. | Why they trust a supplier, what disappoints them, what keeps them loyal, and what could motivate switching. |
| 9. Compare 20 businesses | Start with 20 distinct invitation-related businesses. Compare their operations, material choices, problems and buying habits; keep differences and contradictory examples. | Which needs recur, which belong to a specific client type, and which are still assumptions. Missing public information becomes a question, not a reason to invent an answer. |
| 10. Clarify directly | Use existing customer conversations or opt-in discussions to ask about actual recent purchases, especially details rarely stated publicly. | Who decides, actual quantities and reorder cycles, price sensitivity, supplier relationships and willingness to consider another paper. |

| Client question | Where / how to learn it | How it informs the product approach |
| --- | --- | --- |
| What do they make and for whom? | Business descriptions, finished-product posts, workshop explanations and customer questions. | Separate premium/custom work, volume production, resale and other situations only when the evidence supports those distinctions. |
| Do they buy paper themselves? | Sourcing posts, production descriptions, questions about blanks versus raw sheets, direct conversations. | Approach raw-stock purchasers differently from designers who influence selection or resellers who outsource everything. |
| Which materials do they use, and why? | Named paper/specifications, comparisons, supplier questions and purchase accounts. | Understand whether one of the actual catalog papers matches their work; record alternative materials rather than assuming a match. |
| What causes waste, delays or rework? | Troubleshooting threads, complaints and specific production stories. | Learn whether consistency, printability, dimensions, handling or delivery is the real concern; do not assume our paper solves it. |
| How do they judge price and value? | Quote requests, comparisons and actual buying conversations. | Learn whether they compare sheet price, usable yield, finished-job cost, reliability or another criterion. Public prices may be prices charged to their customers, not paper purchase costs. |
| How much and how often do they buy? | Explicit sourcing quantities, reorder discussions and direct purchase accounts. | Understand small repeated orders versus larger planned purchases; never substitute followers or likes for volume. |
| When do they need stock? | Rush requests, production calendars, prior-season discussions and customer conversations. | Identify purchasing lead times, replenishment patterns and seasonal preparation, especially for Nhung đỏ applications. |
| Who chooses and who pays? | Owner/designer/production/purchasing statements and direct conversations. | Learn whether to explain appearance, production practicality or purchasing economics, and to whom. |
| Why would they change suppliers? | Complaints, recommendations, switching stories and discussions of the current supplier. | Identify a meaningful reason to consider us rather than sending a generic paper advertisement. |
| What would they need to see before buying? | Requests for samples, real finished examples, specifications, availability or delivery information. | Choose the evidence and explanation that answers their actual uncertainty. |
| What words do they use? | Short exact excerpts from relevant posts/comments, kept with source links and dates. | Describe the offer in the clients' language. Keep observed statements separate from our interpretation. |
