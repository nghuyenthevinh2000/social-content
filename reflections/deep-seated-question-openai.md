# Deep-Seated Questions for a Storyteller

These questions are not a checklist to finish. They are tensions to revisit before, during, and after telling a story. Their purpose is to make the story honest enough to matter and alive enough to carry that meaning into another person.

## The Master Question

> What truth am I afraid to say, whose unspoken experience does it name, and what must someone risk without certainty for that truth to be earned?

This question tests four foundations of a captivating story:

- **Honesty:** Am I risking something real?
- **Recognition:** Will someone feel seen?
- **Friction:** What resists the change, and what will it cost?
- **Transformation:** Has the change been earned rather than merely announced?

Truth gives a story meaning. Movement gives that meaning somewhere to go.

## On Honesty

- What am I protecting: the truth of the story, or my image as its storyteller?
- What am I afraid the audience will think of me if I tell this honestly?
- Would I still tell this story if nobody praised, shared, or rewarded it?
- Which detail feels dangerous to include because it reveals the most?
- What necessary truth am I tempted to remove because it exposes too much?
- Where am I hiding behind facts, polish, or clever language?

## On Recognition

- What does my listener feel but struggle to put into words?
- What private fear, desire, grief, or hope does this story give a voice to?
- Can the listener find themselves here, or can they only observe me from a distance?
- Am I trying to make them admire me, or helping them understand themselves?
- If they forget every event, what feeling should remain in them?

## On Necessity and Stakes

- Why must this story be told now?
- Why am I the person who must tell it?
- What did this experience cost me?
- What will it cost the character to remain unchanged?
- What comfortable belief must be surrendered before transformation can happen?
- Where is the moment when everything could have gone differently?

## On Friction and Earned Transformation

- What does the character want badly enough to act for?
- What force, person, fear, or belief prevents them from getting it easily?
- What happens when their usual way of solving problems stops working?
- Where do they fail before they understand what must change?
- What does the story force them to risk, lose, admit, or surrender?
- What choice must they make without knowing whether it is right?
- Does the decisive action belong to the character, or does someone else make the transformation easy for them?
- Does the world reward their growth immediately, or must they continue without validation?
- If I remove the applause, gratitude, explanation, or magical confirmation, can the audience still see that the character has changed?
- Is the ending satisfying because the character earned it, or merely comforting because every uncertainty disappeared?

## On the Opening

- What meaningful question does the first line open in the listener's mind?
- Does the opening create honest curiosity, or promise an intensity the story cannot fulfill?
- How quickly does the listener understand that something meaningful is at stake?
- Am I beginning where the story becomes alive, or making the listener wait through explanation?
- What can I withhold to create curiosity without creating confusion?

## On Movement Through Time

- What changes, resists change, or becomes more costly from one moment to the next?
- What unanswered question pulls the listener forward?
- Where does the tension rise, turn, or release?
- Where does the character's first attempt fail and force a different choice?
- Which moment deserves more time because the listener needs to feel it, not merely understand it?
- Where am I rushing past the emotional consequence of what happened?
- Does each scene deepen the truth, alter the stakes, or change our understanding?
- Does the ending fulfill the emotional promise made by the opening?

## On Craft and Restraint

- Which scene proves the truth I am trying to express?
- Am I making the audience wait, or drawing them forward?
- What sentence or scene am I keeping because I love it, even though the story does not need it?
- What can I remove without weakening the emotional truth?
- What must I protect because removing it would make the story safer but less true?
- Does the ending transform the meaning of the beginning?
- Does the structure embody the story's truth, or does the resolution quietly contradict it?

## On Responsibility

- Is this my story to tell?
- Who might pay the price for my vulnerability besides me?
- Am I representing another person's pain truthfully and with care?
- Can I tell the emotional truth without taking away someone else's dignity?

## The Recurring Self-Test

Before telling the story, ask:

> Is it honest enough to risk something, human enough to make someone feel seen, and difficult enough for its transformation to be earned?

At the opening, ask:

> Have I given the listener a truthful reason to continue, and how soon does that reason appear?

While telling the story, ask:

> What does the character want, what resists them now, and what question carries the listener into the next moment?

While editing, ask:

> Does this serve the story's truth, or the storyteller's ego?

Then ask the inverse:

> Am I removing this because the story does not need it, or because I am afraid to be seen?

After finishing, ask:

> Did the character earn the ending through choice and consequence, or did the story arrange the world to prove them right?

Then ask:

> What uncertainty should remain unresolved because resolving it would betray the story's truth?

Finally, ask:

> Did the ending fulfill the promise of the opening, and what will remain in another human being when my words are gone?

If nobody praised the story, ask:

> What part of it would still deserve to exist?
