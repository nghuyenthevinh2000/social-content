## 1. Core Philosophy

Jasmin Alić (Jay) builds everything on one principle: **connection beats information**.

He does not teach hacks. He teaches that the human behind the post — their clarity, their courage, their specificity — determines whether content moves people or gets scrolled past.

Three beliefs underpin everything he does:

| Belief | Implication |
|:-------|:------------|
| **Authenticity outlasts algorithms** | Never write AI-generated content; it lacks the human resonance required for real connection |
| **Every post is a proof point** | You don't say you're an expert — you prove it through the insight in every post |
| **Top of mind = business** | Consistent, value-first content makes you the first person someone thinks of when they need a solution |

## 2. The Anatomy of a Jasmin Alić Post

### Layer 1: The Hook

**Purpose**: Get the reader in the room. That's it.

The hook is not the post. It does not explain, summarize, or sell. It creates a gap — a piece of missing information that the reader must resolve by reading on.

Hook types he uses:

| Hook Type | Structure | Example |
|:----------|:----------|:--------|
| **Bold statement** | Counter-intuitive claim | *"The day I got fired was the best day of my life."* |
| **Quoted objection** | Someone says something wrong — you correct it | *"Can't one person just handle all of marketing?" ...said by someone who's clearly never done it.* |
| **Rumour / insider signal** | Casual observation that implies exclusive knowledge | *"I wasn't shocked… but still had to share this."* |
| **Contrarian opener** | Challenges a widely held belief directly | *"Posting every day is the worst LinkedIn advice I've heard."* |
| **Provocative question** | Forces internal answer before reading | *"What if your best clients aren't following you yet?"* |

> **Rule**: Write the hook *after* the rest of the post. Only then do you know what you actually need to promise.

---

### Layer 2: The Rehook

**Purpose**: Slam the door. Keep the reader inside.

The rehook is the sentence immediately after the hook. It:
- Justifies or contextualizes the hook
- Addresses the reader's instant skepticism
- Makes a promise for what follows

```
Hook:    "I wasn't shocked… but still had to share this."
Rehook:  "LinkedIn just quietly changed how it ranks text-only posts —
          and most people haven't noticed yet."
```

Without the rehook, readers bounce after the hook. With it, they're committed.

---

### Layer 3: The Body

**Purpose**: Deliver on the hook's promise. Prove the insight.

Body formatting principles:

- **White space is mandatory** — short lines, single-idea sentences, paragraph breaks every 1-3 lines
- **One idea per post** — never dilute. One clear takeaway changes everything.
- **Specificity sells** — replace vague statements with precise numbers, timelines, named results
- **Show, don't tell** — replace adjectives ("I was surprised") with scenes ("My jaw dropped")
- **Active voice** — "She broke the glass" beats "The glass was broken"

Body structures he uses:

| Structure | When to use |
|:----------|:------------|
| **Numbered list** | Teaching multiple related points; scannable, promise-fulfilling |
| **Scene → lesson** | Personal story grounded in a real moment, then the insight extracted |
| **Myth → truth** | State the false belief, then dismantle it with specifics |
| **Pain → Agitate → Solution (PAS)** | Identify reader's pain, make it vivid, then offer the resolution |

---

### Layer 4: The Closing Line

**Purpose**: Make it linger. One line that recontextualizes everything above.

> ❌ *"So that's my story about persistence."* ← summarizes, adds nothing
> ✅ *"The only way out was through — and I walked out the other side."* ← image, earned, stays

The closing should not explain. It should land.

## 3. The Five Post Formats He Uses

### Format 1: The Hook + List
**Structure**: Provocative hook → rehook → numbered list → punch close
**Best for**: Education, authority positioning, scannable insight delivery

```
[Hook — bold statement or common misconception]
[Rehook — context or promise]

Here's what most people miss:

1. [Point — grounded in scene or specific]
2. [Point]
3. [Point]
...

[Closing line — gut punch or recontextualization]
```

---

### Format 2: The Contrarian / Myth-Buster
**Structure**: False belief → why it's believed → why it's wrong → truth → implication
**Best for**: Establishing authority by taking a position, generating comments/debate

```
[Common belief, stated as others hold it]

Most people think this is true.

It isn't.

Here's what's actually happening:
[Specific, verifiable counter-evidence]

The implication:
[What this means for the reader's behavior]

[Closing line]
```

---

### Format 3: The Scene → Insight (Personal Story)
**Structure**: Real scene → tension → turning point → lesson extracted
**Best for**: Building trust, humanizing expertise, making insight memorable

```
[Opening — scene-based, active, specific. No context first.]
[Tension — what was at stake]
[Turning point — the moment things changed]

Here's what that taught me:
[Lesson — specific, applicable to reader]

[Closing line — holds the emotional weight]
```

The **"Dear Son" technique** applies here: draft the post as if writing to your own child. It forces clarity, authenticity, and genuine advice instead of performance.

---

### Format 4: The Insider / Rumour Post
**Structure**: Casual observational hook → specific inside information → broader implication → CTA or punch close
**Best for**: Timely content, algorithm changes, industry news framed through expertise

```
[Hook — casual, observational, implies exclusive knowledge]
[Rehook — the specific thing worth knowing]

[Body — precise details, what changed, what it means]

What this means for you:
[Specific implication for the reader's behavior]

[Closing — no fluff, direct]
```

---

### Format 5: The Proof Point (Authority Post)
**Structure**: Result or specific outcome → how it happened → transferable lesson → invitation
**Best for**: Business development without selling, demonstrating credibility through demonstrated outcomes

```
[Hook — a specific result, not a claim: "6 months of clients booked. Zero sales posts."]
[Rehook — why this matters and is replicable]

What I did instead:
[Specific behaviors, not vague advice]

The principle behind it:
[Transferable insight the reader can use]

[Closing — leaves with something actionable or thought-provoking]
```

## 4. Engagement as Content: The Commenting Playbook

Jay teaches that commenting is often **more powerful than posting**.

| Metric | Post | Comment |
|:-------|:-----|:--------|
| Daily limit | 1-2 posts/day | 20-30 comments/day |
| Discoverability | Algorithm-dependent | Profile visits from every comment |
| Relationship building | One-to-many | One-to-one |

**What a good comment does** (never "Great post!"):

- Adds a new angle the post didn't cover
- Offers a personal take or counter-view
- Summarizes the post's key insight in your own words
- Provides a real-world example that extends the point
- Asks a specific, non-generic question

**Feed management**: He organizes connections into custom lists:
- Prospects
- Peers / Collaborators  
- Influencers in niche
- "Buddies" (engaged regulars)

Each day, comment on 5-10 posts from each list. Each comment is a micro proof point of expertise.

## 5. The "Dear Son" Writing Technique

**How it works**:

1. Write "Dear son," at the top of your draft
2. Write "Love, Dad" at the bottom
3. Write your post in between
4. Delete both lines before publishing

**Why it works**:

- Forces authentic, jargon-free language (you wouldn't give your child bad advice)
- Creates a single, specific imaginary reader — the clearest application of "write to one person"
- Eliminates performance — you're not writing to impress; you're writing to genuinely help
- Speeds up writing because the emotional context is clear

## 6. The Pre-Publish Checklist (Jay's 3 Questions)

Before hitting publish, ask:

1. **Is this helpful to my audience?** — not interesting, not clever. Helpful.
2. **Is what I've written clear or confusing?** — if you have to think to understand it, they'll leave.
3. **Is this helping grow my brand's authority?** — every post is a proof point or it isn't worth posting.

And the formatting check:

- [ ] Does line 1 create irresistible curiosity?
- [ ] Does line 2 (rehook) make the reader commit to finishing?
- [ ] Is there enough white space? Would it fit easily on a phone screen?
- [ ] Is it one idea — not two or three?
- [ ] Does the last line linger, or does it just summarize?
- [ ] Does it sound like a human, not a brand logo?

## 7. What NOT to Do (Failure Modes He Explicitly Teaches Against)

| Failure | What it looks like | Fix |
|:--------|:-------------------|:----|
| Opening with context | "Today I want to share thoughts about…" | Open with conflict, surprise, tension |
| Writing to everyone | "Hey LinkedIn family!" | Pick one reader. Write only to them. |
| Leading with a stat | "78% of marketers say…" | Lead with the human moment behind the stat |
| Explaining the ending | Post concludes by restating the lesson | End on an image, a gut-punch, or a recontextualizing line |
| Passive constructions | "The team was led by…" | Find every "was [verb]ed" and flip it |
| Generic comments | "Great post!" | Add genuine value or don't comment |
| Using AI to write | AI-generated content lacks human resonance | Write it yourself — your imperfection is the proof |
| Sales posts | "Book a call with me!" | Every post proves your expertise — inbound does the rest |
