Research date: **October 7, 2026**. Scope: Stan, Chatbase, Rezi, Kibu, and Web3Forms. Public-source desk research only; no accounts created, paid API calls, customer outreach, or full-dataset crawl.

MRR figures are the snapshots exposed by TrustMRR, not independently audited October revenue. Revenue sync timestamps are the best public freshness indicators located; exact MRR calculation timestamps were not separately exposed. Sources: each company's TrustMRR Markdown profile, linked below.

**Selection caveat:** These are five identifiable entries from the displayed MRR leaderboard, chosen for category coverage. They are not the top five overall. The previously observed leaderboard positions were 1, 2, 4, 8, and 48 respectively; these are UI observations, not independently reconstructed ranks. TrustMRR's separate profile/API `rank` measures a different revenue ranking and must not replace an MRR rank.

**Main lesson:** Revenue evidence and customer identity evidence are separate. Aggregate payment-provider data shows that a business monetizes; it does not prove that any named testimonial author pays, or explain the revenue split between customer segments.

## Evidence rules

- **Provider-backed aggregate:** TrustMRR says the metric comes from payment records. Preserve freshness warnings.
- **Paid-tier association, vendor-reported:** A named customer is explicitly associated with a commercial plan. Stronger than a logo, but not an independently verified payment or contract.
- **Customer relationship claimed:** A case study/testimonial describes use, without establishing payment.
- **Target stated:** Official product/pricing material names the audience.
- **Inferred:** Analyst hypothesis about purchaser, budget, purchase trigger, or retention. Not a measured fact.
- **Search-only lead:** Search extraction was available, but the underlying page could not be fully fetched. Lower-confidence follow-up material, not proof.

No independently verified named payment or contract was found in this bounded pilot. Public user counts, subscription counts, institutional seats, and unique paying customers are not interchangeable.

## 1. Stan — creator commerce

### Product and pricing

Stan supplies a creator storefront with bookings, course delivery, recurring memberships, email collection, community features, and Instagram AutoDM. Creator Pro adds email marketing, funnels, order bumps, affiliate management, payment options, and tracking. This replaces a collection of storefront, booking, course-hosting, and marketing tools. [S2]

- Creator: **$29/month or $300/year**.
- Creator Pro: **$99/month or $948/year**.
- No permanent free storefront plan. Affiliate-only accounts are not free stores. [S2]

### Who pays and why

- **User/target stated:** Creators launching or scaling their own businesses. [S2]
- **Buyer and budget, inferred:** The creator/business owner, using their business operating budget.
- **Purchase trigger, inferred:** First product launch, first coaching offer, or need to convert social-media attention into sales.
- **Renewal reason, inferred:** Active checkout links, courses, customer lists, and marketing workflows make it an ongoing operating tool.
- **Named relationship:** Abigail Peugh's vendor-published story describes selling digital products and using order bumps. It also explicitly describes affiliate income. **Customer relationship claimed; payment not established.** Affiliate affiliation is a conflict-of-interest flag, not a reason to discard the example. [S3]

### Research risks and alternatives

Alternatives to evaluate, not tested here: Gumroad-style digital-product checkout, course platforms, or a separate website plus booking/email tools.

Do not confuse a creator's sales through Stan with Stan's subscription revenue. Do not classify shoppers buying a creator's products as subscribers to Stan's software. Search results for Trustpilot contain both kinds of reviews, plus support/payout complaints; direct review fetch was blocked, so no complaint frequency is asserted. [S4]

**Open questions:** Subscriber retention for new versus established creators; affiliate-referred share; actual paid plan of named creators; identity discrepancy between TrustMRR's listed founder and Stan's public co-founder references. Do not use founder metadata for identity enrichment until reconciled.

### Revenue baseline

Displayed MRR **$3,569,654**; active subscriptions **101,590**; last revenue sync **2026-04-20T15:42:25.479Z**, expired Stripe credential. Historical snapshot, not current October performance. [S1]

## 2. Chatbase — customer-facing AI agents

### Product and pricing

Teams connect knowledge sources, configure agent instructions/actions, test answers, deploy to customer-facing channels, and monitor conversations. The current website advertises support, sales, product guidance, and human helpdesk handoffs. These are vendor-described capabilities, not results of hands-on testing. [C2]

- Free: **50 message credits/month**.
- Hobby: **$40/month**, 700 credits.
- Standard: **$150/month**, 4,000 credits; includes helpdesk, voice/telephony and advanced integrations.
- Pro: **$500/month**, 15,000 credits; Enterprise: custom.
- Annual plans advertise 20% savings. Add-ons: **$40/1,000 recharge credits**, **$25/extra agent/month**, **$99/month branding removal**. Credits are not necessarily completed conversations. [C3]

### Who pays and why

- **User/target stated:** Support/CX teams and businesses needing sales/product guidance. [C1, C2]
- **Buyer and budget, inferred:** A CX/support leader, founder, or operations leader; customer-service/operations budget, with IT/security involvement for larger deployments.
- **Purchase trigger, inferred:** Repetitive ticket volume, after-hours demand, or support workload growing faster than the team.
- **Renewal reason:** Opal's case study describes continuing high-volume recurring support for a small team; the broader retention hypothesis is ongoing support demand. [C4]
- **Named evidence:** **Opal**, explicitly listed on **Enterprise** in Chatbase's customer story. **Paid-tier association, vendor-reported**; actual payment, discounts, and contract value undisclosed. [C4]
- Chuck E. Cheese's CMO and Sage's Product Owner are named in vendor testimonials. These support use, not proof of payment or the identity of the purchasing decision maker. [C2, C3]

### Research risks and alternatives

Compare with staffed support plus FAQ/helpdesk automation, Botpress, or other support-agent platforms; no competitive product test performed.

**Pricing friction observed:** Usage credits and add-ons can materially increase the base price. This is documented pricing, not a proven customer complaint. Competitor reviews surfaced credit-cost complaints but conflicting/outdated numbers, and a Reddit thread included a purported Chatbase employee disputing claims. Do not promote those into established findings. G2 fetch was blocked. [C3, C5]

**Open questions:** Cost per actually resolved conversation; quality/escalation rate; billing with different models; consumer-app versus enterprise revenue mix; current MRR after the disconnected feed.

## 3. Rezi — job-seeker tools and institutional career software

### Product and pricing

Rezi combines resume creation, editing, keyword targeting, scoring, cover letters, and interview practice. Enterprise adds a white-labeled interface, review assignment, team roles, and institution-level administration. [R2, R4]

- Free: one resume, one AI interview, three PDF downloads.
- Pro: **$29/month**, unlimited resumes/downloads and a monthly expert review.
- Lifetime: **$149 once**; does not include the recurring expert review.
- Institutional starter: **$99/month per 200 users**; customized Enterprise offering is separately quoted. [R3, R4]

### Who pays and why

- **Consumer target stated:** Job seekers. **Buyer/budget, inferred:** The individual or someone helping them, using personal spending.
- **Institutional targets stated:** Universities, employment organizations, recruiting agencies, nonprofits, and career sites. **Buyer/budget, inferred:** Career-service/program directors or agency owners using an institutional program/software budget. [R3, R4]
- **Consumer trigger, inferred:** A job search requiring multiple tailored applications.
- **Institutional trigger:** The vendor's customer descriptions emphasize resume-review capacity and consistent formatting. [R4]
- **Renewal hypothesis:** Individuals renew during an active search; institutions serve successive cohorts. A successful job search may end the consumer need, so ongoing B2C retention must not be assumed.
- **Named relationships:** Merit America, Hofstra University, and BMA Group have concrete use descriptions on the Enterprise page. **Customer relationships claimed**, with payment and contract terms undisclosed. [R4]
- **Payment lead:** A search extraction of a Trustpilot review says a subscription was worth the cost for the month needed to find a job. **Self-reported payment, search-only lead**; underlying page returned 403, and reviewer identity was not reliably recovered. Not transaction-verified. [R5]

### Research risks and alternatives

Alternatives to test: a document template plus general AI assistance, other resume tools, or human resume/career services.

**Critical classification correction:** Google's, Amazon's, and similar logos appear under employers where users landed jobs—not companies paying Rezi. The TrustMRR B2C label omits an explicitly marketed institutional business. Enterprise also offers audience monetization/revenue sharing, so institutions can distribute access rather than simply purchasing seats. [R2, R4]

Treat ATS-rejection explanations and headline interview-success rates as vendor claims, not independently established hiring facts. **Open questions:** B2C/B2B MRR split, lifetime contribution to cash revenue, institutional contract terms, post-job-search cancellation, and paid/free access for named customers.

## 4. Kibu — disability-provider operations and educational content

### Product and pricing

Kibu combines provider documentation, attendance, service notes, reporting, member records, medication-related workflows, and educational content/lesson plans. Its homepage addresses organizations supporting people with intellectual and developmental disabilities (I/DD). Public provider pricing is **contact sales**; no contract amount or per-seat price established. [K1, K2]

Legal terms distinguish **organizational Enterprise access under an MSA and Order Form** from **consumer subscriptions purchased directly by individuals or on someone else's behalf**. Consumer plans may renew monthly or annually, but those cancellation/renewal terms must not be applied to institutional contracts. [K4]

### Who pays and why

- **Primary target stated:** Disability-service provider organizations.
- **Users stated:** Direct Support Professionals (DSPs), program supervisors, and directors; supported individuals benefit from the content. [K2, K3]
- **Buyer and budget, inferred:** Program/operations leadership or an executive, using a provider organization's software/program budget; finance/compliance may participate.
- **Consumer payer stated:** The membership holder, which may be a guardian or another authorized purchaser. [K4]
- **Purchase trigger documented in case study:** Woodfords staff needed cohesive educational resources instead of a scattered Google Drive collection. [K3]
- **Renewal hypothesis:** Content becomes part of a daily program routine; documentation tools address recurring work. The Woodfords case study supports content use, not adoption of every current EHR/compliance feature.
- **Named relationship:** Woodfords Family Services, with named program manager, supervisor, and director. **Customer relationship claimed**, no explicit paid amount/plan. [K3]
- ANCOR's partner page says Kibu serves over 40 ANCOR members in 18 states. This is a trade-association partner profile, not an independent customer/payment audit. [K5]

### Research risks and alternatives

Compare with existing provider-management/EHR software plus manually assembled lesson plans, spreadsheets, and shared drives. The latter workflow is directly described by Woodfords. [K3]

No credible independent product-complaint evidence was established in this pilot. Regulatory compliance and HIPAA wording are vendor claims; do not present a website badge as an audit. **Open questions:** Organization pricing, migration costs, actual use of documentation versus content, institutional renewal provisions, and consumer share of aggregate revenue.

## 5. Web3Forms — hosted form backend

### Product and pricing

Website builders add an access key and submit an HTML form to Web3Forms; the service sends submissions to email and offers storage/integrations. It removes the need to build and operate a form backend. Despite its name and a TrustMRR crypto/Web3 category, the researched core product is a contact-form service, not a demonstrated blockchain workflow. [W1, W2]

- Free: **250 submissions/month**, unlimited forms/domains subject to fair use.
- Pro: **$149 billed annually**, advertised as approximately **$12/month**; 10,000 submissions/month.
- Agency: **$399 billed annually**, advertised as approximately **$33/month**; 20,000 submissions/month.
- Special Starter: **$49/year** for eligible students, startups, and nonprofits; 5,000 submissions, with feature restrictions.
- Paid add-ons include linked emails, seats, storage, and branding removal. The fetched view showed annual prices; standalone month-to-month plan prices were not confirmed. [W3]

### Who pays and why

- **Targets stated:** Developers/website owners; an explicit Agency & Team plan targets multi-client operations. [W1, W3]
- **Buyer and budget, inferred:** Website owner, freelancer, agency owner, or development team using a web-operations/client-delivery budget.
- **Purchase trigger documented by pricing:** Exceeding free submission capacity or needing attachments, autoresponders, multiple recipients, integrations, and collaboration. [W3]
- **Renewal hypothesis:** Forms remain embedded in live websites and deliver ongoing inquiries; agencies centralize client forms.
- **Named relationships:** Lucian Tartea/Inovatik and Michael Purcella appear in official testimonials emphasizing free use/free features. **User relationship claimed; paid relationship not established.** [W2, W3]
- A sold-out historical AppSumo listing confirms a lifetime offer existed, not that a particular reviewer is a current paid subscriber. The extraction inconsistently shows a three-review header and an empty review section. No named purchase evidence recovered. [W4]

### Research risks and alternatives

Compare with Formspree, an equivalent hosted form endpoint, or a custom backend; no hands-on comparison performed.

**Documented operational limit:** After reaching the submission cap, new submissions stop until the next month or an upgrade. This can be a business concern, not an established complaint. Historical lifetime offers and old competitor comparisons make it unsafe to map every user to today's subscriptions. [W3, W4]

**Open questions:** Paid agency versus individual mix, lifetime-user share, deliverability under real traffic, and named current subscribers. The TrustMRR B2C label does not adequately capture the official agency offering.

## Pilot assessment and scale recommendation

### What worked

- Five dossiers explain the offer, buyer hypotheses, pricing, purchase triggers, and evidence gaps.
- All five have provider-backed revenue snapshots; only two have recently synced payment data.
- Official sources disclosed mixed buyer models that simple directory labels miss.
- Customer evidence ranges from a named paid tier (Opal) to testimonials explicitly about free features (Web3Forms).

### What remains unproven

- Exact current MRR ranking across all eligible startups.
- Named transaction-verified paying customers and customer-segment revenue shares.
- Measured retention, willingness to pay, and complaint prevalence.
- Independent product performance/security verification.

### Before researching 100

1. Obtain authorized TrustMRR API/MCP access. Its public AI endpoints are fixed snapshots, not the full top-MRR dataset. [Access policy](https://trustmrr.com/llms.txt)
2. Paginate the eligible dataset and sort locally by `revenue.mrr`. The documented `revenue-desc` sorts last-30-day revenue, not MRR; list pages currently allow at most 10 records. [List API](https://trustmrr.com/docs/api/list-startups)
3. Freeze the selection and preserve anonymous entries; explicitly flag zero/missing MRR and stale feeds. Separate the site's displayed ranking from any freshness-qualified analysis.
4. Use the same source labels and distinguish consumer, institutional, agency, and marketplace-side payers.
5. Research in batches of 10. Escalate weak named-payment evidence or contradictory pricing; do not require public customer identities where none are available.
6. Keep direct-page evidence separate from search-only leads, affiliate/partner material, and competitor-authored comparisons. Never interpret blocked review pages as no complaints.

**Recommendation:** The format is usable for 100-company research, but ranking access and revenue freshness need resolution first. No further companies or paid requests are authorized by this pilot approval. No opportunity is yet validated strongly enough to call an underserved market; potential interviews should test creator retention, support-agent total cost, job-search churn, provider procurement, and agency form-management needs.
